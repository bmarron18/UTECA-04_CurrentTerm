							A QUICK LOOK AT HUMOR


Humor can be categorized into various styles and genres, which can be broadly classified based on their content, delivery, and psychological impact. Common types include:

---	I Psychological Styles of Humor	--- 
Psychologists often categorize humor based on its use and effect on well-being. 
    * Affiliative Humor: Used to enhance relationships and bring people together. It involves genial, inoffensive joke-telling and witty conversation, aiming to amuse others and reduce tension.

	* Self-Enhancing Humor: A coping mechanism used to maintain a cheerful outlook and regulate emotions during adversity. It involves finding humor in one's situation and not being overwhelmed by stress.
 
 	* Aggressive Humor: Involves humor at the expense of others, using sarcasm, teasing, ridicule, or disparagement. This style is often used to manipulate or criticize others and can be detrimental to relationships.
	
    * Self-Defeating Humor: Involves excessive self-disparagement or "putting oneself down" to gain approval or acceptance from others. This can be associated with poor psychological well-being. 


--- II Theatrical and Performance Genres	---
In performance and literature, humor is also categorized by form and content. 
    ** Satire: Uses wit and irony to critique or mock societal shortcomings, institutions, or human folly. Examples include works like A Modest Proposal by Jonathan Swift.
    
    ** Farce: A form of low comedy that uses highly improbable situations, exaggerated characters, and chaotic plots for amusement.
    
    ** Slapstick: Relies on physical actions, pratfalls, and exaggerated violence for laughs (e.g., The Three Stooges).
    
    ** Romantic Comedy: A lighthearted plot centered on the development of a romantic relationship, typically ending with a happy conclusion.
    
    ** Dark Humor: Finds humor in morbid or taboo subjects such as death, crime, or other sensitive topics. The humor arises from the discomfort and absurdity of the situations.
    
    ** Deadpan: A form of delivery where humor is presented without emotion or a change in facial expression, forcing the audience to find the humor in the statement itself. 
    
    ** Comedy of Manners: A form of high comedy that uses sophisticated language, wit, and subtle characterizations to satirize the behaviors and conventions of a particular social class.


---	III Other Specific Styles	---
    *** Observational Humor: Focuses on everyday life and social norms, pointing out the humor in common situations and shared experiences. Jerry Seinfeld's act is a classic example.
    
    *** Absurdist/Surreal Humor: Focuses on illogical events and bizarre situations, often defying rational explanation. Monty Python is a prime example.
    
    *** Anti-Humor (Anti-Joke): Intentionally unfunny or a joke with an anti-climax, where the humor comes from the expectation of a joke that never materializes.
